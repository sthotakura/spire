# Sample products

The builder has a menu of sample products, grouped by family. Each opens a complete, valid product, and each has its own address, `?example=<slug>`, so a link opens the builder on that product. The samples are listed in `src/content/examples.ts`.

## Decisions

- **Modelled on public products, with generic names.** Each sample reproduces the structure of one public product recorded in the research notes, using only concepts the reference already explains. The menu names the structure generically, never the issuer or the product's own title; the ⓘ beside it names them. Underliers are synthetic indices starting at 100, and principal is 1,000.
- **The source behind an ⓘ.** While a sample is open, an ⓘ beside the menu names the public product it is modelled on, links to its filing, and says how the sample differs from it, so a sample reads as a real structure rather than an invented one. The title, link and difference are kept with each sample in `src/content/examples.ts`, and a test checks that the table below names the same title and link. The ⓘ keeps its place when no sample is open, so opening one shifts nothing. The hint states that the filing is shown for its structure, not as a recommendation.
- **Curated, not generated.** The menu does not list the schema's examples in `scripts/product-examples.ts`; those exist to exercise each feature of the structure. A sample is added by hand when a public product can be reproduced in full.
- **Beside the page heading, not in the Structure panel.** Opening a sample replaces the whole page: the sentence, outline, chart, calculation and JSON. Inside the panel it read as one of the product's terms. Beside the heading it takes no extra height; below 900px it wraps under the intro.
- **Grouped.** Market-linked deposits, participation notes, digital return notes, barrier absolute return, autocallable notes and issuer callable notes, as `<optgroup>`s of one select.
- **Terms only.** An address names the product's terms, not a scenario. Opening a sample returns every scenario input to where a first visit starts (a final level of 110, a basket's assets each up 10%, no review levels, closes or issuer call set by hand), and every term the sample leaves out to its first value, so the menu and the address open it the same way.
- **The address follows the terms.** Once the reader changes a term, `?example` is removed and the menu returns to its placeholder, so the address never names a product the builder does not show. Choosing a sample replaces the address rather than adding a history entry, so Back leaves the builder.
- **No address, no sample.** A visit with no `?example` opens the starting note, as before. An address naming no sample opens the starting note and drops the slug.
- **Linked from the book.** Each sample is linked from the chapter that explains it, by its address on the published site, which the book view opens within the app.
- **A query, not a path.** `/spire/?example=…` is served by `index.html` with HTTP 200 on Pages. A path such as `/spire/examples/…` would be served by `404.html`, as a book chapter is.

## The samples and their sources

Where a filing gives a range set on the pricing date, the sample takes the low end. The terms the research notes did not record were re-read in the filings on 18 October 2026: the three Morgan Stanley PDFs as text extracted from the files, and the others through a summarising fetch tool. The Citigroup and BMO digital return notes were re-read as text extracted from their filings on 24 October 2026; neither states a range, so the samples take the stated terms. The two BofA notes and the Bank of Nova Scotia note were read the same way on 24 October 2026, with the formulas, which the filings print as images, read from the images. The two market-linked CDs are as recorded in [deposit.md](deposit.md). The three Morgan Stanley notes link to the EDGAR copies of the same preliminary pricing supplements, checked on 21 October 2026 against the PDFs for their CUSIPs and ranges; they price on 27 October 2026, after which the final supplements can replace them. The CDs are bank deposits, not registered securities, so they have no EDGAR filing and keep Morgan Stanley's links.

## Public products not used

- The Morgan Stanley basket CD averages 28 quarterly closes; the reference averages at most 12 dates.
- The JPMorgan lookback buffered note reads the lesser performing of two indices; lookback is not available on a basket.
- The BofA digital return plus notes on the worst of two metal funds ([424B2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1682472/000191870426018506/form424b2.htm)) pay a 96.5% digital payment from 100% of the starting values, principal between 80% and 100%, a one-for-one fall below 80%, and 150% of the rise beyond 196.5% of the starting value. Participation that starts above the initial level is not a feature of the reference.
- The Morgan Stanley Jump Securities and the JPMorgan digital barrier note pay a fixed digital return, which is modelled now ([digital-return.md](digital-return.md)), but their terms have not been re-read for a sample.
- The two GS Finance barrier absolute return notes pay a minimum return inside the barriers, which barrier absolute return does not model.
- The HSBC barrier absolute return note repeats the JPMorgan one with barriers set on the trade date.
- The BofA fixed coupon autocall needs a fixed coupon. Shark fin notes are described only in educational material.
