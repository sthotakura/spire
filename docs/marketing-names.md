# Marketing names

A marketing name is the label a seller puts on a note, such as "principal-protected note". It is not a contract term and no single authority defines it. Two notes with the same payoff can carry different names, and one name can cover slightly different payoffs. SPIRe shows names as hints beside the structure the reader built. The structure stays the authoritative description.

Generic, publicly used names appear here, and for a rise shared at more than 100%, the titles issuers put on their filings, since that is the only way such a note is sold under a name. Those titles are the issuers' own, some are service marks, and each reason says whose it is. They appear only so a reader can recognise a filing; SPIRe is not connected with those issuers. Other branded names stay out.

## Established (from the sources)

| Term | What the source says |
| :--- | :--- |
| Full principal protection | FINRA: some notes "are designed to provide 100 percent, or full, principal protection if held to maturity". |
| Partial principal protection | FINRA: "other structured notes offer only partial principal protection, such as 10 percent". SSPA's glossary defines *Partial Capital Protection* as "between 90% and 100% of the nominal value". The two sources draw the line differently. |
| Participation rate, cap | FINRA lists "participation rates, caps on upside performance or floors on downside performance" as features and defines none of them. SSPA's glossary entry for *Participation* (German text in the English PDF, translated here) says the investor profits from the underlier's performance "1:1, over- or under-proportionally". SSPA groups products that follow the underlier under the category *Participation*. *Capped Participation*: "The product has a maximum yield." |
| Capital Protection Note with Participation (1100) | SSPA: "Minimum redemption at expiry equivalent to the capital protection", with "Participation in underlying price increase above the strike". Capital protection is "a percentage of the nominal (e.g. 100%)". |
| Tracker Certificate (1300) | SSPA: "Reflects underlying price moves 1:1". |
| Outperformance Certificate (1310) | SSPA: "Disproportionate participation (outperformance) in positive performance above the strike", and 1:1 when below the strike. |
| Leveraged or enhanced participation with a cap | The SEC bulletin describes notes with a leveraged or enhanced participation rate "only up to a capped, maximum amount" (search excerpt). |
| Market-linked CD, structured deposit | Morgan Stanley: market-linked CDs are "time deposit obligations" that pay "$1,000 for each CD … plus a supplemental amount" based on an index. MiFID II: a structured deposit is a deposit "which is fully repayable at maturity" with interest paid "according to a formula". |
| Buffer | FINRA: "a buffer typically provides 'hard protection' such that if the buffer level is breached, an investor's potential principal loss is restricted to the extent of losses in excess of the buffer". "Buffered" is also the plain descriptive word US offering documents filed with the SEC use in note titles. |
| Bonus Certificate (1320) | SSPA: "Participation in performance of the underlying"; "Minimum redemption is equal to the nominal provided the barrier has not been breached"; "If the barrier is breached, the product changes into a Tracker Certificate"; "multiple underlyings (worst-of) allow for a higher bonus level or lower barrier". |
| Bonus Outperformance Certificate (1330) | SSPA: "Disproportionate participation (outperformance) in positive performance above the strike"; the same minimum redemption provided the barrier has not been breached; "If the barrier is breached, the product changes into a Outperformance Certificate". |
| Twin Win Certificate (1340) | SSPA: "Profits possible with rising and falling underlying"; "Falling underlying price converts into profit up to the barrier"; minimum redemption equal to the nominal provided the barrier has not been breached; "If the barrier is breached, the product changes into a Tracker Certificate". The rates are not stated. |
| Snowball autocall | uSMART: the "Snowball coupon is periodically accrued on the basis of previous coupon level by a specific rate and is paid out contingent on the occurrence of a Knock-out (“KO”) event", as "Coupon (%) x (number of months lapsed from the issue date / total number of months in a calendar year) x notional amount"; the KO event is a close "at or above the KO barrier" on a KO observation date. ConvexTrade adds a knock-in barrier "typically set at 60–80% of the initial spot price", and says on a call "the investor receives principal plus all accrued coupons". The two describe the downside differently. |
| Autocallable note with step-up premium | HSBC: "On each observation date that the note is not called, the Call Premium increases, Steps Up, by a predefined percentage", after "a predetermined non-call period". Its hypothetical terms state the premium as "10.5% per annum, 5.25% semi annually", with call premiums of 10.50%, 15.75%, 21.00% and 26.25% on reviews six months apart from month 12, and "Principal + Final Call Premium" at maturity at or above the call threshold. Its final premium is printed as 31.00%, where the steps give 31.50%. |
| Shark fin note | Educational articles describe a capital-protected note that participates in a rise up to a knock-out barrier, with a "rebate" paid if the barrier is reached, built from a zero-coupon bond and an up-and-out call. They describe the barrier as observed on any trading day, using the closing level. |
| PLUS | Morgan Stanley, No. 9,577: "$1,000 + leveraged upside payment" on a rise, at a 300% leverage factor, up to a "Maximum payment at maturity"; "$1,000 × index performance factor" otherwise, with "Minimum payment at maturity: None". "Performance Leveraged Upside Securities" and "PLUS" are its service marks. |
| Trigger PLUS, Dual Directional Trigger PLUS | Morgan Stanley: below "the trigger level", "$1,000 × index performance factor"; "without any buffer". The dual directional form pays "$1,000 + ($1,000 x absolute index return)" from the trigger level up to the initial level. Excerpts show a Trigger PLUS with a maximum payment; the dual directional filing read has no upside cap. |
| Buffered PLUS | Morgan Stanley (excerpts): a 200% leverage factor, a 10% buffer, a minimum payment of 10% of principal and a maximum payment of 128.54%. A minimum equal to the buffer means a rate of 100% past it. |
| Accelerated Return Notes | BofA: "a leveraged return, subject to a cap", at a 300% participation rate, and "1-to-1 downside exposure". The ending value averages five closes. "Accelerated Return Notes" and "ARNs" are its registered service marks. |
| Enhanced Return Notes | BofA (excerpts): upside exposure on the least performing underlying at rates from 112.5% to 185%; principal at or above the threshold value, and "1:1 downside exposure" below it. No cap appears in the excerpts. |
| Return Enhanced Notes | JPMorgan: "Capped Return Enhanced Notes", "$1,000 + [$1,000 × (Index Return × Upside Leverage Factor)], subject to the Maximum Return", and 1% lost for every 1% of fall, with no buffer. Deutsche Bank uses the same words in a title. |
| Buffered Return Enhanced Notes | JPMorgan: a 1.26 upside leverage factor and a 15% buffer, with "$1,000 + [$1,000 × (Index Return + Buffer Amount) × Downside Leverage Factor]" past it. The downside factors found (1.1765, 1.25, about 1.11) are 1 ÷ (1 − buffer). Titles come as "Capped" and "Uncapped". |
| Accelerated Barrier Notes | JPMorgan: "an uncapped return of at least 1.70 times any appreciation", principal at or above the barrier amount, and "$1,000 + ($1,000 × Index Return)" below it, read on one date. |
| Leveraged Market-Linked Step Up Notes | Barclays: at or above the starting value, "the greater of (a) $10 + the Step Up Payment and (b) $10 + a leveraged return equal to 124.00%" of the rise; below it, a loss of principal. |

## Rules for SPIRe

These are example-specific assumptions about when a name fits the model's terms, not definitions from the sources. Several names can apply to one note. A note with no protection, no participation and no cap gets no name.

| Rule (model terms) | Shown name | Vocabulary | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Protection is 100% | Principal-protected note | US descriptive | With upside participation, SSPA calls this a Capital Protection Note with Participation. |
| Protection is above 0% and below 100% | Partially principal-protected note | US descriptive | Any level in between counts as partial, following FINRA's example of 10%. SSPA's narrower 90%–100% definition is noted in the hint. |
| On a bullet note with no coupon: upside rate is 100%, downside rate is 100%, no protection, no buffer, no cap | Tracker | SSPA (Tracker Certificate) | 1:1 in both directions. |
| The same | Delta One | Market usage (the titles of public Morgan Stanley notes and a Barclays update; no regulator or SSPA name) | The same payoff under the name those documents use. The filings read do not define it, so the hint gives the general meaning of delta. See [adjustment-factor.md](adjustment-factor.md). |
| On a bullet note with no coupon: upside rate is above 100%, downside rate is 100%, no protection, no buffer, no cap | Outperformance | SSPA (Outperformance Certificate) | Leveraged or enhanced upside in US descriptive wording. |
| On a bullet note with no coupon: upside participation selected, no protection, no buffer, no cap, and neither of the two rules above applies | Participation note | SSPA (category *Participation*) | Covers upside alone, and upside with downside at other rates: for example upside 80% with downside 100%, or upside 100% with downside 90%. Downside participation alone gets no name. This is a category label, not a product type from the SSPA map, so it is the least specific name here. |
| On a bullet note with no coupon: a barrier on downside participation, downside rate 100%, upside rate 100%, no protection, buffer, cap, upside barrier, absolute return or digital return | Bonus certificate | SSPA (1320) | Principal is repaid while the barrier is not reached, and a reached barrier makes it follow the underlier one for one, as the Swiss Tracker. The reason says the Swiss barrier is observed during the life, and that one read on the final date differs; it also says when the initial level is a lookback level, which the Swiss type does not have. |
| The same, with upside rate above 100% | Bonus outperformance certificate | SSPA (1330) | As above, with the leveraged upside. |
| The same, with absolute return on the fall above the barrier | Twin-win certificate | SSPA (1340) | The Swiss text does not state the rates, so the reason states this note's. A buffer instead of a barrier gets no such name: the SSPA definition is the barrier form. |
| On a note: a digital return | Digital return note | Market usage (the word in the titles of public pricing supplements from four issuers; no regulator or SSPA name) | Shown for any redemption. The other maturity names end their reason by saying they do not cover the digital return, and it rules out Tracker, Outperformance and Participation note, since it changes what the note pays. |
| On a note: upside rate above 100%, with any other features | Leveraged participation note | US descriptive (SEC bulletin) | Describes the upside only, so a fall does not matter. The bulletin mentions it with a cap; this rule does not need one. A deposit gets none, as for the other note names. |
| Cap is present with upside participation | Capped participation | US descriptive, SSPA glossary | The cap is a maximum return on principal. |
| Deposit with upside participation | Market-linked deposit | US descriptive (market-linked CD), EU and UK (structured deposit) | Principal is repaid by the wrapper, so a deposit gets none of the note names above. |
| Buffer is present with downside participation | Buffered note | US descriptive | Without downside participation the buffer has nothing to absorb, so it gets no name. |
| On a note: upside participation and an upside barrier, no downside participation, and protection below 100% or absent | Shark fin note | Market usage (educational articles; no regulator or SSPA name) | With no downside participation a fall leaves principal unchanged. A rebate is optional. Shown for a barrier observed on the final date or on every close, since the name follows the payoff diagram. The reason says sellers observe the barrier on every trading day, so the rebate is paid if the underlier ever closed above it; for a barrier observed only on the final date it adds that the contract differs. |
| On a note: protection is 100%, upside participation and an upside barrier (downside participation does not matter, since the floor bounds it) | Shark fin PP | Market usage (PP for principal protected) | Shown in place of the plain name, beside Principal-protected note. Partial protection keeps the plain name, since PP would overstate it. |
| On a note: the redemption is an autocall | Autocallable note | US descriptive (FINRA glossary), SSPA glossary *Auto-Callable* | Any payoff, since FINRA says the feature appears in many product types. |
| On a note: an autocall whose call amount is per year and above zero | Snowball autocall | Market usage (a broker's product description and a glossary; no regulator or SSPA name) | A call pays the call amount × months elapsed ÷ 12, the knock-out payment in the broker's description. Shown beside Autocallable note, for any payoff and with or without a coupon, since the name follows the growing call payment. The reason says some sellers' snowballs also have a barrier observed on every close, and that this note's payment below the trigger at maturity is as its payoff states. An issuer call gets no snowball name: both sources describe a call on a level. |
| On a note: an autocall whose call amount is per year and above zero | Autocallable note with step-up premium | Market usage (an issuer's product title; no regulator or SSPA name) | Shown beside Snowball autocall, under the same rule: the two names describe the same growing call payment. The reason says it is not a step-up note, whose step-up is a fixed return at maturity. |
| On a note: the redemption is an issuer call | Callable note | US descriptive, SSPA glossary *Callable* | The reason says the issuer may call and need not. |
| On a note: a contingent coupon | Contingent coupon note | US descriptive (issuer titles), SSPA glossary *Conditional Coupon* | The reason adds memory when the coupon has it, and says US documents also call it contingent interest or contingent income. |
| On a note: an autocall, a contingent coupon, a downside barrier, no buffer, no upside participation, no protection | Express certificate | SSPA (1260 Express-Barrier-Certificate) | The return is the coupons. | On a note: protection is 100% and a contingent coupon, no upside participation | (reason of Principal-protected note) | SSPA (1140) | The reason names the Capital Protection Certificate with Coupon, as it names 1100 for upside participation. |
| On a note: absolute return in both directions, with a lower and an upper barrier | Barrier absolute return note | Market usage (the product title in pricing supplements from five issuers; no regulator or SSPA name) | The reason says the payment is principal plus the absolute value of the return, a rise or a fall alike, until a barrier on either side is reached, and then a fixed return. A deposit gets none of the note names. |

### Issuer titles for a rise shared at more than 100%

All of these need a note with upside participation above 100%, no upside barrier, no minimum return, no call, no coupon, and cash settlement, since the filings pay that way. PLUS, Accelerated return note and Return enhanced note describe the upside, so they apply whatever a fall does: with no downside participation, another downside rate or protection, the note keeps the name and its reason says the filings read lose principal one for one, so the contract differs. Every filing read does put principal at risk. The titles that name a downside shape need that shape and no protection. The vocabulary is `Issuer title`. Several can apply at once, beside the names above. A cap is stated in the reason when the note has one. Where the filings read are all capped, or all uncapped, a note that differs keeps the name and its reason says the contract differs, as the shark fin reason does for its barrier.

| Downside (model terms) | Shown names | Cap on the filings read |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Any or none, no buffer or barrier | PLUS, Accelerated return note, Return enhanced note | All capped |
| Rate 100%, a buffer | Buffered PLUS | All capped |
| Rate 1 ÷ (1 − buffer), a buffer | Buffered return enhanced note | Either |
| Rate 100%, a barrier read on the final date | Trigger PLUS | Either |
| The same | Accelerated barrier note, Enhanced return note | None capped |
| Rate 100%, a buffer, absolute return on a fall | Dual directional buffered PLUS | Either |
| Rate 100%, a barrier read on the final date, absolute return on a fall | Dual directional trigger PLUS | Either |
| Rate 100%, no buffer or barrier, a digital return at a 100% barrier on the final date, no cap | Leveraged market-linked step up note | Not capped; a capped note gets no name |

These are example-specific assumptions. The titles do not depend on how the levels are read, so an averaged or lookback level, and a single underlier or a basket, keep them, although BofA's filings read average the final level and its Enhanced Return Notes are worst-of. A barrier read on every close gets no title, since every filing read tests it once. "Enhanced return" (BofA, with a barrier) and "return enhanced" (JPMorgan, without one) are different products, so they stay separate.

With upside participation measured from the digital return ([participation-above-digital.md](participation-above-digital.md)), the Leveraged market-linked step up note is not shown, since that title pays the greater of the two, and the Leveraged participation note says what the leverage reads. No generic public name for this form was found, so it has none of its own.

Tracker, Outperformance and Participation note name a product that only follows the underlier to maturity, so an autocall, an issuer call or a coupon rules them out. The three Swiss barrier names (Bonus, Bonus outperformance and Twin-win) are ruled out the same way, and by a digital return. The other names that describe the payment at maturity still apply. When the product has a call or a coupon, each of their reasons ends by saying so: the call can end the product earlier, and the name does not cover the contingent coupon. The names for the call, the coupon and the Express certificate carry their own reasons and no such sentence. A digital return rules out the same three names, since it changes what the note pays, and ends each other maturity name's reason with a sentence saying the name does not cover it. It has a chip of its own, Digital return note, as issuers title notes with it.

A protection of 0% counts as no protection. The two pay the same, and the documentation already treats "absent" and "0%" as different descriptions of the same payment.

## Categories

The SSPA groups products into four categories: Capital Protection, Yield Enhancement, Participation and Leverage (which holds warrants and similar products). A category is a broad class with one answer per note, unlike the names above, of which several can apply. SPIRe uses the SSPA's word "Capital Protection", not "capital guarantee", since the issuer's credit risk remains.

| Rule (model terms) | Category | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Protection is above 0% | Capital Protection | Partial protection counts, at any level. SSPA's own partial protection is 90%–100%; the hint says so. Protection decides first, so a buffer or a cap does not change it. |
| Deposit with upside participation, no protection term | Capital Protection | The wrapper repays principal in full. Example-specific assumption. |
| On a note: no protection, a coupon (fixed or contingent), no upside participation | Yield Enhancement | With or without a call. The SSPA puts the Express and reverse convertible types here; both pay a coupon in place of upside. Example-specific assumption for a bullet note with a coupon. |
| On a bullet note with no coupon: no protection, upside or downside participation | Participation | Includes buffered and capped notes. Downside participation alone is a category here although it gets no name. |

Leverage is not shown, since it needs warrants, which the model does not have. Protection decides first, so a protected note with a coupon is Capital Protection, as SSPA's Capital Protection Certificate with Coupon (1140) is. An unprotected note with a call and no coupon, or with a coupon beside upside participation, shows no category. Only the matching category is shown; the others are not listed. Downside participation alone is Participation by the rule but gets no name, so the category is not shown for it. A note with no protection and no participation (for example the barrier absolute return note) shows no category, since placing it is unverified.

## Interface

The category is not shown on its own. Each name's popup ends with a line "Category: …", so it is read with the name it sits beside, and a note with no name shows no category. The names appear as chips under the summary sentence, after the label "Often marketed as". Selecting a chip opens a short reason and highlights the part of the note it rests on. A name that rests on one part selects that part. A name that rests on several selects the whole payoff. The rules live in `src/content/names.ts`.

## Open questions

- Whether the barrier absolute return note and an unprotected shark fin note belong to Participation, Capital Protection or a category of their own is unverified, so the first shows none and the second shows Participation by the rule above.

- SSPA's Outperformance Certificate is described without a cap. Whether a capped, leveraged note carries "Outperformance" in its name is unverified, so only "Capped participation" is shown for it.
- With upside participation and no downside participation selected, the model repays principal on a fall. Whether that is a "principal-protected" structure or only resembles one is undecided. The rules look only at the protection term, so a note with upside participation alone is not called principal-protected.
- US usage has no formal taxonomy comparable to SSPA's. The US descriptive names above are plain-language labels from regulator material, not a defined list.
- The open questions on the names for a call and a coupon are in [call-and-coupon-names.md](call-and-coupon-names.md). Apart from the shark fin note, the Express certificate and the barrier reverse convertible, names for structures with barriers are not shown. The shark fin chip carries a caveat only when the barrier is observed on the final date; with daily close observation the reason says the rebate is paid once any close touched the barrier. It should gain a rule if a regulator or issuer definition of the name is found.
- A note with both a buffer and a protection floor shows both names. No generic name for the combination was found.
- Whether Buffered Return Enhanced Notes also exist at a rate of 100% past the buffer is unverified, so a buffer at that rate shows only Buffered PLUS.
- Whether Enhanced Return Notes and Accelerated Barrier Notes are ever capped is unverified. Enhanced Return Notes rest on search excerpts, and a filing should be read in full if the name gains more weight.

Names describe how a structure is commonly presented, not what it pays. Payoffs remain illustrations of contractual payments under stated assumptions, not valuations, investment advice, or guarantees of issuer payment.
