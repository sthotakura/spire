# Boundaries of the reference

SPIRe explains how explicitly stated terms produce contractual maturity
payments in synthetic scenarios. Its examples cover equity and equity-index
references, a single underlier or a basket (weighted, worst-of or best-of), and
the determination and payoff rules described in this book.

## Contractual payment and market value

The illustrated payment is not the price at which a product could be bought
or sold today. Market valuation would require additional information and a
pricing model. The payoff diagrams and scenario tables supply neither.

Repayment also depends on the party that owes it. A contractual floor,
buffer, or barrier does not demonstrate that an issuer can meet its obligations.

## Observations and cash flows

The examples use hypothetical observed levels. They do not connect to live
market data. Observation counts stand in for schedules; actual calendar
dates and business-day conventions are not calculated. A term is a whole
number of months, from 1 to 120 in this reference.

The products illustrated pay at scheduled maturity or when an autocall or an
issuer call ends them early, and an autocall can also pay a contingent coupon on
its reviews. Reviews are numbered and evenly spaced, not dated, and a payment is
made at its review. An issuer call is the issuer's choice, stated by a scenario,
and pays principal and any call amount, with no coupon in it. A fixed coupon, and
a return a year over payments made on several dates, are not calculated. A
barrier can be observed on every close, but only through the one lowest or
highest close a scenario states. It is not combined with an autocall or an
issuer call. Conditions observed on calendar dates would need
dated observations, which are not modelled.

## Reading other contracts

A familiar label does not establish an identical rule. Check the underlier,
determination method, threshold equality, observation period, rates, maximums,
and floors. For a basket, establish how component returns are combined.

Fees, taxes, dividends, currency conversion, corporate actions, and early-sale
proceeds are outside the worked examples. This reference is a way to learn
the stated mechanics, not a substitute for a product's contractual documents
or advice about an investment decision.
